# MOFU Playbook — Middle of Funnel

Contents: 1. Stage reality · 2. Psychological triggers · 3. Format matrix · 4. CTA rules · 5. Banned moves · 6. Patterns

---

## 1. Stage Reality

**Audience state**: Solution-aware. They know they have a problem, are actively comparing options, and your product is on their mental shortlist (or should be). Trust is the deciding variable — they are evaluating whether you specifically are the right choice.

**Content job**: Reduce doubt, deepen trust, and help them see your solution fits their specific situation better than the alternatives. Education about the category is over — they know the space. Now they need evidence and fit.

**Success signal**: Email opens and clicks, demo requests, content downloads, free trial starts, webinar attendance — micro-commitments, not purchase yet.

---

## 2. Psychological Triggers

| Trigger | When to use | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Social proof (specific)** | Testimonials, case studies | Real name/company/role + before-and-after with numbers + the specific problem that was solved. "Great product!" is not social proof. "Dari 14 jam/minggu jadi 3 jam — kami mulai pakai ini waktu tim kami tumbuh ke 8 orang" is. |
| **Authority transfer** | Expert content, media mentions, comparisons | Third-party validation transferred to the buying decision. Name the specific expert, media, or certification — generic "experts agree" is invisible. |
| **Objection pre-emption** | FAQ, nurture email, comparison page | Name the 3 real objections your warmest leads raise (get these from sales calls, not assumptions). Answer each with evidence, not reassurance. "We understand your concern" is not an answer. |
| **Specificity cascade** | Demo, how-it-works content | Every claim followed immediately by a concrete example. "Powerful" becomes "reduces [X task] from [Y time] to [Z time], which we've seen across 47 accounts in our beta." If numbers are estimates, label them. |
| **Loss framing (mild)** | Retargeting, follow-up email | The specific opportunity cost of not acting — a concrete scenario of what happens next month without a solution, not a generic fear statement. |

---

## 3. Format Matrix

| Format | Primary trigger | MOFU-specific execution |
|---|---|---|
| Email nurture sequence | Trust-building arc | 3–5 email structure: (1) value/insight → (2) case study → (3) objection handler → (4) comparison or demo → (5) soft offer. Each email is self-contained. |
| Comparison / "vs" page | Objection pre-emption + authority | Be honest about 1–2 criteria where competitors win — audiences at MOFU are doing their own research; caught spin destroys trust permanently. |
| Case study | Social proof (specific) | Problem → specific approach → quantified result + one unexpected benefit. Include a quote that sounds like a real human, not a brochure. |
| Webinar / live demo | Authority transfer + specificity cascade | Show the product solving the audience's stated problem scenario, not a generic "here's our dashboard" demo. |
| Retargeting ad | Loss framing + social proof | References the specific content they already saw or the specific product page they visited. Feels like a logical next step, not a new pitch. |
| FAQ / knowledge base | Objection pre-emption | Searchable. Each answer: 1–3 short paragraphs. Link to deeper proof (case study, demo). Not essay-length defenses. |

## 4. CTA Rules

**Allowed**: "Download the full case study", "Watch the 10-minute demo", "Join the live webinar", "Compare plans", "Get the checklist", "Start your free trial" (low-friction SaaS), "Book a 20-minute call" (if low-pressure framed).

**Forbidden**: Generic "Learn more" (too soft — they already know the category); hard "Buy now / Beli sekarang" close (too fast before objections are handled).

**Why**: At MOFU, the ask should feel like a logical next step in the relationship, not a jump. Each CTA should advance commitment without demanding it.

---

## 5. Banned Moves

- Re-running TOFU awareness content to a warm audience (they know the category — repeating it wastes their attention and signals you don't know who you're talking to).
- Social proof without specifics: "Customers love it", "Highly recommended", "Banyak yang sudah puas" — replace every one with a real outcome or labeled placeholder.
- Making unverifiable claims about competitor products.
- Overselling pressure: if a MOFU prospect senses urgency pressure, trust erodes and they leave. Trust content must not feel like a pitch.
- Generic email subject lines without context: "Quick question", "Just checking in", "You forgot something" — these work only when the sender is known and the context is clear.
- Long feature lists without outcome framing — they know what features exist; tell them what those features do for their specific situation.

## 6. Patterns

**Case study structure**:
`[Client profile: role, company size, industry — 1 line]. [The exact problem they had, named as they described it]. [What was done — approach, not achievement]. [Quantified result + one unexpected benefit they didn't anticipate]. [A quote that sounds like a real human: specific, slightly rough, not polished-brochure.]`

**Objection-handler email**:
- Subject: "[The objection, word-for-word as a prospect would say it]"
- Opening: "I hear this every week." — acknowledges the objection as legitimate, not as a mistake.
- Body: Reframe with evidence (a specific case or data point). Do not argue against the objection; redirect to a different frame.
- CTA: One proof asset — case study, demo, or trial. Not a purchase.

**Comparison page rule**:
List 3 criteria your audience actually uses to decide (get from sales call recordings). Be honest on the criteria where competitors are stronger; explain the trade-off and why your approach is still the right choice for your specific audience. This honesty builds the trust that closes the sale.

**Nurture email arc** (5-email):
1. Value: insight that reframes their problem (no product pitch)
2. Social proof: one case study, one specific result
3. Objection handler: "The most common reason people wait to [take action] — and why it usually costs them more"
4. Comparison or demo: positioned around their specific situation
5. Soft offer: "When you're ready, here's the next step" — low pressure, specific CTA
