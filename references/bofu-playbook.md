# BOFU Playbook — Bottom of Funnel

Contents: 1. Stage reality · 2. Psychological triggers · 3. Format matrix · 4. CTA rules · 5. Banned moves · 6. Patterns

---

## 1. Stage Reality

**Audience state**: Product-aware, comparison-complete, ready to decide. They've done the research, narrowed their shortlist, and need the final push. The barrier is friction and residual doubt — not awareness, not education.

**Content job**: Remove the last objection, eliminate friction, and make the action feel safe, smart, and immediate. Every word that does not serve the conversion is a distraction.

**Success signal**: Purchase, sign-up, signed contract, paid invoice — the conversion event itself.

---

## 2. Psychological Triggers

| Trigger | When to use | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Real scarcity / urgency** | Deadline offers, limited inventory or cohort | Only when the constraint is real and verifiable. Fake scarcity at BOFU is detected by an audience that has been researching you — and it permanently destroys trust. Real urgency (actual deadline, actual seat count) is among the most effective conversion levers. |
| **Risk reversal** | Guarantee, free trial, first session free | Shifts perceived risk to the seller. The stronger and more specific the guarantee, the lower the buyer's hesitation. "Satisfaction guaranteed" means nothing; "If you don't [specific outcome] in [timeframe], we refund the full amount — no questions" is a risk reversal. |
| **Reciprocity** | Final bonus stack, upgrade offer | A genuinely valuable final incentive (not cheap add-ons) tips the decision for someone on the fence. Must be perceived as an unexpected gift, not a discount to compensate for lack of value. |
| **Identity confirmation** | "People like you" testimonials | Show buyers who have the same role, company size, and problem succeeding with your product. The prospect sees themselves in the outcome. Curate testimonials for profile match, not just positive sentiment. |
| **Loss aversion (strong)** | Cart abandonment, final email | Concrete cost of inaction: the specific scenario of what next month, next quarter, next year looks like without solving this problem. Not generic fear — a specific, plausible, consequential scenario. |

---

## 3. Format Matrix

| Format | Primary trigger | BOFU-specific execution |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page (sales/offer) | Risk reversal + identity confirmation | One goal, one CTA, all objections handled on-page. Remove every exit link. Every section earns its place by removing a reason not to buy. |
| Cart abandonment email sequence | Loss aversion + risk reversal | 3-email arc: (1h) friendly reminder + direct link; (24h) top objection handler + strongest testimonial; (48–72h) risk reversal + real deadline if applicable. |
| Testimonial / results gallery | Identity confirmation | Curated by audience profile match: same industry, same company size, same problem — not just best quotes. |
| Retargeting ad (offer) | Real scarcity + identity confirmation | Most targeted messaging in the funnel. References the specific product, specific offer, specific deadline. No new education. |
| Proposal / quote follow-up (B2B) | Risk reversal + loss aversion | Ties the proposal back to the client's stated pain in their own words. One-page summary. One clear next step. |
| Checkout page copy | Risk reversal + identity confirmation | Every word is about safety, speed, and "you made the right call" — not features. Trust badges, guarantee reiteration, social proof in the sidebar. |

---

## 4. CTA Rules

**Format**: Direct, specific, single action. One CTA per conversion surface.

**Examples**: "Start your free 14-day trial", "Claim your spot — [X] left", "Get [Product Name] now — cancel anytime", "Sign the proposal".

**Urgency**: Allowed only when real. "Offer ends [real date]", "Only [real number] of spots remain" — must be verifiable. Never "ending soon" without a date.

**Forbidden**:
- Two competing CTAs on one page ("Buy now" + "Learn more" side by side).
- "Maybe later" as a secondary option — it is a conversion exit, not a fallback.
- Soft language: "consider", "might be right for you", "you could" — replace with direct outcome language.
- "Learn more" on a page where the audience came ready to decide.

---

## 5. Banned Moves

- Educational content at BOFU: explaining the category or the problem is wasted copy on an audience that completed research.
- Fake countdown timers or false "Only 3 left!" claims — BOFU audiences are skeptical, have researched you, and will test the counter by refreshing. Caught = trust destroyed.
- Long feature lists without outcome framing — they know the features; show what those features produce in their specific context.
- Missing risk reversal — at BOFU, no guarantee is perceived as high risk = no conversion.
- Mismatched CTA tone: a "Let's explore if this is a fit" CTA on a sales page loses the prospect who arrived ready to decide.
- Fabricated testimonials or made-up results — at this stage the prospect may verify them.

## 6. Patterns

**Sales page headline formula**:
`[Desired outcome], [timeframe or context], [without the audience's main objection].`
Example shape (not a template): "Klien pertama kamu dalam 30 hari — tanpa harus cold DM orang asing."
Derive the outcome, timeframe, and objection from the product's actual user data or explicit user input.

**Cart abandonment email arc**:
- Email 1 (1h after): Simple, warm, direct link back. "Kamu ninggalin sesuatu" — no pressure, no manufactured urgency.
- Email 2 (24h after): Address the #1 abandonment reason for your product (price? commitment? timing?). Strongest specific testimonial. One CTA: back to checkout.
- Email 3 (48–72h after): Risk reversal stated clearly + real deadline if applicable. If no real deadline: make the risk reversal the main push, not fake urgency.

**Risk reversal frame**:
`"Kalau kamu [tidak mencapai hasil spesifik] dalam [timeframe yang konkret], [tindakan yang akan kamu lakukan — refund, perpanjang, redo]. Kami sudah melakukan ini untuk [jumlah nyata atau 'semua customer yang meminta']."`
Never vague. Auditable beats impressive.

**Proposal follow-up (B2B)**:
Line 1: Restate their pain in their words (from the discovery call or brief).
Line 2: Name the one decision this proposal answers.
Line 3: Risk reversal + one specific next step with a deadline.
Body: The proposal itself — no new education, only decision-support.
